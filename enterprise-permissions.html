PROJECT OVERVIEW

Who should be allowed to open a company file?

The problem: giving everyone the same access can expose information to people who do not need it.

What I did: designed a permission model that checks a person’s job role and the conditions of the request, such as time, device, or location.

Why it matters: the right person gets the access needed for the task, while other requests can be refused. This follows the idea of giving people only the permissions they need.

01 / PERMISSION MODELS

Two models. Complementary strengths.

RBAC and ABAC comparison
AspectRBACABAC
Decision basisPermissions assigned to a user's role.Policy evaluation using user, resource, action, and environmental attributes.
ExampleAn HR Manager may read employee records.Access depends on working hours and a company device.
StrengthClear responsibilities and role-based administration.Context-sensitive, fine-grained authorization.
Design challengeRole definitions and assignments need maintenance.Attributes and policies need careful management.

03 / REPRESENTATIVE USE CASES

HR records

An HR Manager can access employee records based on the assigned job role.

ABAC example

Employee report

An employee can access a report only during working hours and from a company device.

Decision inputs

Time and device attributes.

Hybrid example

Manager access

The user's role must be Manager and the connection must be through company Wi-Fi.

Role plus network context.

04 / BENEFITS & CHALLENGES

Flexibility needs careful governance.

Intended benefits

  • Limit inappropriate access to enterprise resources.
  • Combine clear roles with situational authorization.
  • Support least-privilege permission design.
  • Make access requirements easier to explain and document.

Design challenges

  • Define reliable roles, attributes, and policies.
  • Keep permission administration manageable.
  • Account for systems with limited policy support.
  • Maintain accuracy as roles and conditions change.
