Technical research and presentation

The Quantum Revolution — Introduction to Quantum Computing

Making a complex computing topic understandable through its foundations, applications and engineering limits.

My contribution

Research, comparison and a collaborative Project Day presentation to a visiting ABET committee.

01

The problem

Quantum computing is easy to describe as a universal speedup, yet its usefulness depends on the problem, algorithm and system constraints. The challenge was to explain the core concepts and practical implications clearly to a professional audience.

02

Evidence I analyzed

  • Research covered qubits, superposition, entanglement, quantum algorithms, error correction and system limitations.
  • Classical and quantum approaches were compared, alongside possible applications in cybersecurity, optimization, healthcare and data analysis.
  • The presentation organized the findings for Project Day; this was conceptual research rather than hardware experiments or performance benchmarking.
03

What I did

  1. Organize the basic quantum concepts into a clear introduction.
  2. Compare quantum and classical computation without assuming every task benefits.
  3. Connect possible applications to error correction and practical system limitations.
  4. Present the research with a teammate to a visiting ABET committee during Project Day.
04

Why this approach

I explained applications together with limitations so the audience could distinguish research potential from demonstrated capability. Familiar comparisons helped introduce the ideas, while the technical discussion retained the roles of algorithms, noise and error-correction overhead.

05

The result

The outcome was a collaborative research presentation delivered to a professional audience. It demonstrated structured technical research and communication of complex engineering concepts. No quantum computer, algorithm benchmark or operational security migration was implemented.

06

Practical impact

Without this approach

A broad claim that quantum computers speed up everything makes it difficult to judge where the technology matters and what preparation is relevant.

With this approach

The project gives a more informed way to discuss opportunities and limits. For example, IT teams can frame future cryptographic readiness as a planning question. That is practical context for the research, not a migration completed by this project.

The demonstrated impact is research communication and technical understanding. Healthcare, optimization and security outcomes are possible application areas, not measured achievements of this project.

IN PRACTICE

Think of encryption as a lock on digital information.

Future quantum computers could weaken some types of digital locks. Organizations can prepare by identifying those locks and planning suitable replacements.

Find the digital locks

Identify where applications and services use cryptography to protect information.

Decide what needs attention first

Consider sensitive information and the systems that depend on each lock.

Check replacement options

Test whether newer protection methods work with the existing applications and services.

01 / FOUNDATIONS

The concepts behind the shift.

01

Qubits

The basic unit of quantum information. Measuring a qubit in the computational basis returns 0 or 1.

02

Superposition

A quantum state can combine basis states through amplitudes. Measurement does not reveal every possible result at once.

03

Entanglement

Some joint quantum states cannot be described as independent states of their individual qubits.

04

Interference

Quantum algorithms manipulate amplitudes so outcomes can reinforce or cancel.

02 / ALGORITHMS & LIMITATIONS

Potential depends on the problem.

Algorithmic thinking

Structure matters

Quantum algorithms use quantum effects deliberately. A different processor alone does not make every computation faster.

Engineering challenge

Errors & noise

Quantum information is fragile. Error correction encodes logical information across physical qubits to help protect it.

Practical evaluation

System constraints

Hardware quality, circuit depth, and error-correction overhead shape which computations are feasible.

This project researched and explained quantum computing. It does not claim a quantum hardware implementation or a demonstrated performance advantage.

Educational context: IBM · Fault-tolerant quantum computing

Cryptographic resilience

Sufficiently capable quantum computers could threaten some public-key cryptography. Post-quantum cryptography uses resistant algorithms on classical computers.

Computational possibilities
